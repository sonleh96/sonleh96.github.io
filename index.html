Highlights

Sen4x: free satellite imagery at 2.5 m

Asian Development Bank, 2024 to now

Sentinel‑2 photographs the whole planet every few days for free, but at 10 m per pixel. That is too coarse for buildings, informal settlements or small farms. Sen4x gets it to 2.5 m.

The first version, published in IEEE JSTARS, fused repeated passes with a prior learned from Pléiades Neo imagery. The follow-up scales it to the whole globe. It fuses eight revisits, then a diffusion transformer adds texture, and each user decides per task how much of that texture to accept.

We judge it by what it does for real mapping tasks. In Manila, a settlement model scores an F1 of 21.5% on Sen4x output against 8.0% on plain Sentinel‑2.

Publications

  1. Beyond pretty pictures at global scale: task-tunable super-resolution of Sentinel-2 imagery

    Retnanto, A., Mueller, S., Le, S., Schindler, K., & Iddawela, Y. In preparation, 2026.

    • Project page

    A two-stage system that lets users set the balance between faithful and sharp super-resolution at inference time. A fusion stage combines eight Sentinel-2 revisits into a 2.5 m image. A diffusion transformer then adds texture, starting from a noised copy of that image, and the noise level sets how much of it is kept. Trained on 1.8 million Sentinel-2/Satellogic pairs from 123 countries and tested on eight downstream tasks, it beats SATLAS and LDSR-S2 on 77,428 held-out scenes while hallucinating less on average.

  2. Beyond pretty pictures: combined single- and multi-image super-resolution for Sentinel-2 images

    Retnanto, A., Le, S., Mueller, S., Leitner, A., Riffler, M., Schindler, K., & Iddawela, Y. IEEE JSTARS, vol. 19, 2025. Also ADB Economics Working Paper No. 823.

    • Paper
    • arXiv
    • ADB working paper
    • Code

    Sen4x combines single-image and multi-image super-resolution. It fuses repeated Sentinel-2 acquisitions of the same place with a prior learned from very high-resolution Pléiades Neo imagery, and outputs 2.5 m imagery. We evaluate it on a downstream mapping task instead of on visual quality alone, where it beats state-of-the-art super-resolution baselines. The title is the point: images that only look sharper can produce worse maps.

  3. Air pollution and rice crop health: evidence from Vietnam’s Mekong Delta

    Go, E., Iddawela, Y., Le, S., & Tan, E. S. Scientific Reports, vol. 16, art. 34813, 2026.

    • Paper
    • Project page
    • Data & Code

    We use instrumental variables and geospatial panel data to estimate how air pollution affects crop health and rice productivity in Vietnam’s Mekong Delta, combining remote-sensing measurements with agricultural data to produce evidence that policymakers can use.

  4. Managing flood risks: leveraging finance for business resilience in Malaysia

    World Bank and Bank Negara Malaysia, 2024. Contributor: climate resilience screening and asset-level geospatial analysis.

    • Paper
    • World Bank page

    A joint World Bank and Bank Negara Malaysia study of how floods affect Malaysian businesses and the financial sector. It estimates that a 1-in-20-year flood could cost Malaysia up to 4.1% of GDP in 2030. My part was the climate resilience screen that joins flood hazard layers to asset registries.
